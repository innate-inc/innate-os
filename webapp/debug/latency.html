How to read it

  
    
    
  • Bars overlap on purpose. The robot starts speaking at the first sentence
      boundary, so Cartesia and the speaker are busy while Gemini is still streaming.
      A stacked chart would have to invent an order that does not exist.
    • 
    
  • Grey bars are the robot's own plumbing — queues, thread handoffs, the
      wait between an event landing and the loop picking it up. Coloured bars are a
      vendor round trip or the speaker.
    • 
    
  • Trailing silence is not shown. The clock starts when the VAD declares the
      utterance over, which is already stt_vad_silence_secs (default 0.5 s)
      after you stopped talking. That constant is a real part of what a person feels;
      add it mentally, or lower it in settings and re-measure.
    • 
  


  
Sim is not hardware

  
    
    
  • Sim TTS holds the whole clip back. On hardware, audio streams straight
      into aplay, so Cartesia first byte is when the speaker starts
      making sound. In sim there is no audio device: the brain collects the entire clip
      and publishes it on /tts/audio for the browser. The page labels that
      bar differently and does not pretend the two are comparable.
    • 
    
  • Sim mic adds a hop. The browser captures, encodes base64 PCM, and pushes it
      over rosbridge before the robot's VAD ever sees it — plus the page's own 800 ms
      VAD tail. Hardware listens with a local ALSA device.
    • 
    
  • Everything between agent wake and Gemini stream is the same code in
      both, so that middle section is comparable.
    • 
  


  
Backends

  
    
    
  • The batch STT backends (elevenlabs_batch, gemini — batch
      is the default) endpoint on the robot, so their internals are visible.
    • 
    
  • The realtime backends (elevenlabs, openai) endpoint
      inside the vendor's WebSocket session. Nothing before the transcript is
      observable, and the page says so rather than drawing a bar it cannot measure.
